## What the Assessment Is Really Measuring

They are not asking “How smart are you?” or “Can you do research-level ML?”

The core question is:

"Can this person become productive as an AI Builder without months of extra training?"

So prepare to show practical judgment, reading-and-reasoning skills, and the ability to apply familiar tools under time pressure.

---

## Skill Areas to Expect

1. Computational Thinking

- Break problems into steps, spot patterns, reason about algorithms and complexity.

2. Programming Fundamentals

- Reading and reasoning about variables, loops, functions, arrays, strings, simple debugging.

3. Practical AI Usage

- Prompt engineering, common AI failure modes (hallucinations), model limitations, and productivity workflows (ChatGPT, Copilot, Claude, etc.).

4. Problem Solving & Troubleshooting

- Real-world debugging, diagnosing performance issues, and choosing practical fixes.

---

## What They Are NOT Likely Testing

- Deep math (calculus, linear algebra proofs)
- Building neural networks from scratch
- Compiler internals, OS kernels, or advanced database tuning

Focus your study time on applied knowledge and judgment instead of specialist theory.

---

## How to Think During the Assessment (A 4-step Routine)

When you read a question, run these four quick checks:

1. What is the question actually asking?
2. Which options are obviously wrong — eliminate them.
3. Have I seen something similar before? (pattern match)
4. If I were building this in real life, what would I do?

Use elimination and practical instincts to raise your odds quickly.

---

## Common Traps (and how to avoid them)

- Reading too fast — watch for words like NOT, ALWAYS, NEVER.
- Changing correct answers without new reasoning — trust your first answer unless you find a clear mistake.
- Ignoring keywords (best, most likely, first, last).
- Overthinking simple questions — the simplest answer is often the right one.

---

## Time Management Tips

- If you have 60 questions in 60 minutes, aim ~1 minute per question.
- Quickly answer easy questions first and mark hard ones to revisit.
- Use elimination to reduce choice space — every saved minute compounds.

---

## Hands-on Study Strategy (Use this every chapter)

1. Read the explanation.
2. Re-type the example — don’t copy/paste.
3. Attempt every practice question (even guesses help learning).
4. Read the provided explanation.
5. If you get one wrong, write one sentence: Why it was wrong.

This active loop (do → check → reflect) accelerates retention.
