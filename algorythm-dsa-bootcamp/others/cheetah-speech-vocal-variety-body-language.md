# The Cheetah
### Toastmasters Level 1 Project: Introduction to Vocal Variety and Body Language

---

## Version 1: Refined Speech (Full Script, Delivery Time 5.5 to 6.5 Minutes)

Good evening everyone.

The jungle is a place of constant tension. Every creature there lives by one simple rule: eat or be eaten. Scientists divide the animals of that world into two groups. There are the prey, gentle grazers like antelopes and gazelles, who spend every waking moment listening, watching, sensing danger before it arrives. Their whole existence is built around one instinct: the wiser you are to danger, the more likely you are to survive.

Then there are the predators, lions, tigers, leopards, hyenas, the rulers of that hierarchy. These are the ones who chase, who catch, who kill.

Today I want to tell you about one predator in particular. It is smaller than a lion. It does not roar like a tiger. And yet it is the fastest land mammal on this earth. I am talking about the cheetah.

I first became fascinated with this animal while watching a documentary on National Geographic. If you saw a cheetah without knowing what it was, you might describe it simply as a bigger cat, spotted fur, sleek body, quiet grace. And you would not be entirely wrong. Even its scientific classification places it among the wildcats. But do not let that beauty fool you. Beneath that graceful exterior is one of the most dangerous hunters in the wild.

A cheetah can reach speeds of ninety six kilometers per hour. Sit with that number for a second. In the time it takes you to blink twice, this animal has already covered the length of a football field. There is nothing else walking on land that can match that kind of speed.

Now picture this with me. An antelope grazes quietly on the open plain, unaware that danger is closing in. Somewhere in the tall grass, the cheetah crouches low, muscles coiled, eyes locked on its target. Then, without warning, it moves. It bursts forward like an arrow released from a bow, and the chase begins.

What amazes me is that this speed is not just raw power. It is engineering. The cheetah's spine is not rigid like ours. It bends and stretches like a spring, storing energy with every stride and releasing it forward in one explosive motion. Its long tail works exactly like the rudder of a boat, keeping it balanced as it twists and turns, matching every sudden move the antelope makes to try to escape.

I found myself holding my breath watching this unfold. The antelope darts left. The cheetah adjusts. The antelope cuts right. The cheetah matches it, step for step, closing the gap with every stride. Two of nature's finest creations, one built for survival, one built for the kill, locked in a battle measured in seconds.

And then it is over. The cheetah lunges, grips the antelope by the neck, and brings it down. Just like that, the hunt ends, and the cheetah has earned its early morning meal.

But here is what fascinates me most about this animal. Despite being feared across the wild, despite being one of nature's most efficient killers, history tells us that human beings have never fully tamed the cheetah. Think about your dog or your cat at home. You feed it, you play with it, you throw a ball and it runs to bring it back to you. That bond exists because we tamed those animals over generations. But the cheetah, for all its grace and beauty, has resisted that same closeness with us.

Imagine sitting beside a tamed cheetah the way you would sit beside your own dog. Some of us would love that kind of closeness with such a magnificent creature. Others, myself included, might struggle to shake off the fear.

And maybe that is the lesson hiding inside this story. Speed does not come from size. It comes from design, from every part of a creature working together toward one purpose. The cheetah does not out muscle its prey. It out engineers it, spine, tail, focus, all moving as one. Perhaps that is true for us too. Our greatest strength rarely comes from being the biggest or the loudest in the room. It comes from how well every part of us, our mind, our voice, our body, works together toward one goal.

That, my friends, is the cheetah for you. Fast beyond imagination. Deadly beyond doubt. And still, after everything, wild at heart.

Thank you.

## Delivery Notes

**Timing**: Read at a natural pace, this script runs close to six minutes, right inside your five to seven minute window.

**Where the marks are concentrated**: The chase sequence (antelope and cheetah) and the closing reflection are where you should feel the most contrast. Fast and breathless during the chase, slow and deliberate during the reflection.

**Pick your evaluation focus**: The project asks you to tell your evaluator in advance whether they should score vocal variety, body language, or both. Given how much movement and pacing this speech asks of you, you are well positioned to ask for both, but if you would rather build confidence in one area first, vocal variety is the safer starting point since the content already leans heavily on pace and pitch.

**Rehearse in stages**: First read the plain script out loud without any of the cues, just to get comfortable with the words. Then read the annotated version and let the cues guide your body naturally rather than performing them mechanically. Record yourself once you feel ready and compare it against the Speech Profile evaluation criteria in the PDF.

## Version 3: Introduction, Body, and Conclution

**Introduction**

The jungle is a place of constant tension. Every creature there lives by one simple rule: **eat or be eaten**. Scientists divide the animals of that world into two groups. There are the prey, gentle grazers like antelopes and gazelles, who spend every waking moment listening, watching, sensing danger before it arrives. Their whole existence is built around one instinct: the wiser you are to danger, the more likely you are to survive.. Then there are the predators, lions, tigers, leopards, hyenas, the rulers of that hierarchy.. They attack. They chase. And they kill. 

Today, I want to center my conversation around one particular predator. It is smaller than a lion. It does not roar like a tiger. And yet, it holds one incredible title: **The fastest land mammal on Earth.** The cheetah. Have you ever watched a documentary on National Geographic or any wildlife channel? you may have probably seen this beautiful creature. If you saw a cheetah without knowing what it was, you might describe it simply as a bigger cat with dark spots covering its body. And honestly... you wouldn't be completely wrong. Because the cheetah does look like a cat. But do not let that beauty deceive you. Beneath that graceful exterior is one of the most dangerous hunters in the wild.

---

**Body**

The cheetah can reach speeds of about **96 kilometres per hour**. In the time it takes you to blink twice, this animal has already covered the length of a football field. There is nothing else walking on land that can match that kind of speed. Lets say you're watching the animal standing quietly one moment... and then, within a few moments... **BOOM!** It launches forward. Its body stretches. Its legs move rapidly. Its tail helps it maintain balance as it changes direction. And its spine is flexible, allowing its body to extend and contract as it runs. 

Now imagine that you are watching all of this happen. You see the cheetah begin its chase, bursting forward like an arrow released from a bow. And ahead of it... there is an antelope. The antelope starts running. The cheetah runs faster. The antelope changes direction. The cheetah follows. And at this point, you are no longer just watching animals. You are watching a contest. Speed against survival. Prey against predator. And as I watched the chase, my mind kept racing too. *Who is going to win?* The antelope is running for its life. But behind it is this powerful, fast predator. The cheetah keeps sprinting. Faster... and faster... and faster. And finally, after the chase, the cheetah catches the antelope and brings it down. Just like that, the chase is over. For the cheetah, it is time for its early-morning meal. And when you think about it, there is something fascinating about this animal. The cheetah is one of the most feared predators in the wild. Yet, interestingly, human beings have been able to tame cheetahs.

---

**Conclusion**

Just imagine your ordinary cat or dog. You give it food. It comes closer to you. You play with it. You throw something, and it runs after it. Now imagine doing something similar... with a cheetah. A cheetah. That same animal that can run at incredible speed. That same animal that we just watched bring down an antelope. Imagine having one sitting calmly beside you. I don't know about you... but even if the cheetah was completely tame... That's not Mudi, I can't do that. Because, after all... **a cheetah is a cheetah.**

And maybe that is the lesson hiding inside this story. Speed does not come from size. It comes from design, from every part of a creature working together toward one purpose. The cheetah does not out muscle its prey. It out engineers it, spine, tail, focus, all moving as one. Perhaps that is true for us too. Our greatest strength rarely comes from being the biggest or the loudest in the room. It comes from how well every part of us, our mind, our voice, our body, works together toward one goal.

## SHOPPILOT — 2 MINUTE PITCH SCRIPT

Hi, my name is Mudi Muritala, and I'm the founder of ShopPilot.

Across Nigeria, millions of small business owners still track their sales with pen and paper. It's slow, it's easy to make mistakes, and if that notebook is lost, years of business history disappear with it. When these owners try to access funding or support, they often have no clean record to show for their hard work.

ShopPilot solves this through WhatsApp, a platform they already use every day. A shop owner can type or send a voice note the same way they'd message a friend, "I sold five bags of rice for fifteen thousand naira," and ShopPilot logs it instantly and confirms it back to them. Over time, they get real insight into what's selling and where to focus.

This matters because most digital tools ask people to learn something new. ShopPilot meets business owners exactly where they already are.

Over the next 12 weeks, my expectation is to move ShopPilot from an idea to a tested concept. Success for me looks like speaking with real business owners, sharpening the product based on what they need, building a working MVP, and leaving this program with something I can confidently take to my first users and future investors.

I'm ready to put in the work, and I'd love to do it with the Founders Lab.

Thank you.
